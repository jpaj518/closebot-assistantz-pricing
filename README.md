# closebot vs assistantz ai：voice vs text, full pricing breakdown, and which white-label setter fits your agency

Most people searching this comparison aren't asking which AI writes better sentences. They're trying to work out whether the thing they're about to resell to clients needs to answer the phone.

That's the real fork in the road. CloseBot is an agentic conversational AI that lives inside your CRM and handles text conversations. Assistantz AI is a white-label voice and chat platform that plugs into GoHighLevel and gives you a phone number that talks. Both let you rebill usage. Both let you put your logo on the dashboard. Only one of them picks up when someone calls.

## The short version

|  | CloseBot | Assistantz AI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core job | Qualify leads and book appointments over text channels in your CRM | White-label AI voice calls plus chat across SMS, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, email and web |
| Voice calls | Not supported — text-only | Inbound and outbound voice, 7¢/min with your own OpenAI key |
| Native integrations | HighLevel, HubSpot, LeadConnector, custom CRMs | GoHighLevel sub-accounts, deployed white-labeled |
| White-label | Yes, on the Agency plan, with client portal rebilling | Yes, on every listed plan |
| Entry price | $0 free plan, then $64/mo (business) | $197/mo (Agency Starter) after a paid trial |
| Usage model | Business: messages bundled in the plan. Agency: $0.012 per message, rebillable | 7¢ per voice minute with BYOK, 2¢ per chat message with the vendor's key, free with BYOK |
| Best fit | Agencies and businesses already running text-based lead flow in a CRM | Agencies whose clients lose leads to missed calls |

If your client's pain is "we miss calls after hours", CloseBot alone doesn't fix it. If the pain is "leads sit in the inbox for six hours before anyone replies", a voice platform is the wrong purchase. That's the honest starting point.

## Voice is the line that splits these two tools

CloseBot is explicit about its scope: it takes over all text-based channels inside your CRM, and its own integration list runs HighLevel, HubSpot, LeadConnector and custom CRMs. A third-party comparison of the two product categories notes CloseBot is text-only with no voice or video, and a competitor's write-up makes the same observation. Nothing in CloseBot's pricing page, help docs or feature list offers a phone number.

Assistantz AI goes the other direction. Its listed capabilities include making and receiving calls, plus chat on SMS, Instagram DM, Facebook Messenger, WhatsApp, email and website chat, running in 25+ languages including English, German, Japanese, Spanish, French, Portuguese and Hindi. The vendor positions it as a white-label platform for GoHighLevel agencies, with assistants installed into client sub-accounts without Zapier, n8n or custom API work.

For an agency selling "AI receptionist" services, that difference is the whole product. For an agency selling "AI setter that qualifies your form fills and DMs", the chat side matters more, and that's where CloseBot's design shows up: an objective-driven agent with tools, connector support, a testing portal, and Smart FAQ that flags questions it can't answer confidently and then re-engages every lead who asked once you answer it.

Everything in the list columns is identical across the three tiers — voice AI calling, conversational AI on SMS, Instagram DM, Facebook Messenger, WhatsApp and email, internal GHL white-label deployment, rebilling on voice minutes, both of your own keys, 25+ languages, support docs and monthly Q&A calls. What you're buying with the higher tiers is sub-account headroom.

Two things about the pricing that don't line up neatly:

1. **The FAQ on the homepage says plans start at $97/month.** The plan cards on the get-started page start at $197/mo. The $97 figure may refer to something narrower, but as published, the two numbers disagree.
2. **The trial terms conflict too.** The get-started page offers 14 days for a $27 one-time setup fee, described as non-refundable. The FAQ describes a 30-day trial with $10 in AI call credits and no credit card required, alongside the same $27 non-refundable setup fee.

Usage pricing is where the pitch lives: 7¢ per voice minute with your own key, 2¢ per chat message on the vendor's key, free if you bring your own. You supply the Twilio number and the OpenAI key, which means those costs sit outside the subscription — good for transparency, less good if you wanted a single predictable bill.

The vendor markets 7¢/minute as the lowest voice rate in the market. Independent white-label voice roundups list competing platforms advertising around $0.09 to $0.12 per minute, so the claim looks directionally fair, though "lowest" is a marketing position rather than an audited figure.

## Where each tool actually wins

**Assistantz AI is the better buy when:**

- Your clients' problem is missed phone calls, not slow text replies. This is the single biggest reason to pick it.
- You want raw cost visibility. Bringing your own OpenAI key and Twilio number means you see the invoice, not a bundled rate.
- You need a fixed sub-account count. Three, ten or unlimited, priced flat. No per-message escalation, no segment accounting.
- You're selling a voice-first offer and closing deals on the phone.
- You want to rebill phone numbers as a line item. Assistantz tracks minutes, messages and numbers, and lets you set your own markup.

**CloseBot is the better buy when:**

- The conversation quality is the product. CloseBot's agents split a single thought across short, separately timed messages, offer time windows rather than reading slots off a calendar, retry a booking when the calendar throws an error, and ignore emoji reactions. A third-party review of CloseBot describes that texting style as the strongest part of the tool, and it's the sort of detail that decides whether a lead replies or ghosts.
- You need reasoning, not branching. CloseBot describes itself as agentic with objectives, knowledge and tools, plus industry tooling like live property data and drive-time checks for real estate and home services, Stripe payment collection in the conversation, and custom connectors.
- You're multi-CRM. HighLevel is the deepest integration, but HubSpot and custom CRMs are on the list. Assistantz is GoHighLevel-specific.
- You want a free tier that lasts. 100 messages a month, forever, no card. Assistantz's entry point is a paid trial with a non-refundable setup fee.
- You need HIPAA. CloseBot's Growth tier carries HIPAA compliance and quarterly audits; the vendor also advertises HIPAA and GDPR compliance and 99.99% uptime, which matters in healthcare and dental.
- You're reselling to clients who already live in a CRM inbox. This is the architecture CloseBot was built for.

## The rebilling math, using published rates

Assume a client generates 2,000 chat messages in a month and takes 300 minutes of AI voice.

On CloseBot's agency track, those 2,000 messages cost you $24 at $0.012 each, and you set whatever markup you want when billing the client. Message volume on the business track is bundled instead, which is why a business with predictable volume can end up paying less than the per-message model would suggest.

On Assistantz, the same 2,000 messages cost $40 on the vendor's OpenAI key — or nothing on the platform side if you bring your own key, since you'd then pay OpenAI directly. The 300 voice minutes run about $21 at 7¢/minute, again with your own key, plus whatever Twilio charges for the number.

Neither number is your full cost. You're still paying for the CRM underneath — a GoHighLevel subscription is commonly listed at $97/month for Starter, $297 for Unlimited and $497 for Agency Pro — plus seats ($5 each on CloseBot) and any extra storage.

## What neither tool includes

- **Model costs, handled differently.** CloseBot bundles message costs into business plans and charges agencies per message. Assistantz assumes you supply the OpenAI key and the Twilio number. Different risk profiles, not clearly better or worse.
- **The CRM.** Both sit on top of GoHighLevel in practice, and that subscription is yours.
- **Overages and add-ons.** CloseBot charges a 2× overage rate from your wallet on business plans and $5 per extra seat. Agency storage runs $0.006 per MB per day. This is the kind of line that quietly eats margin.
- **Refunds.** CloseBot states there are none. Assistantz's setup fee is described as one-time and non-refundable. Trial periods are where you find out.

## Five questions that settle it faster than any demo

1. Does the offer you're selling include answering the phone? If yes, stop here. CloseBot doesn't do voice, and bolting a separate voice vendor onto it means two systems, two bills and two places to debug.
2. How many text messages does a typical client generate? If it's under 500 a month, CloseBot's $64 tier bundles them and the per-message model never bites. If it's 20,000, you're in agency territory either way.
3. Do you want one predictable invoice or raw cost pass-through? Assistantz puts the AI and telephony bills in your name. CloseBot keeps you on a bundled rate.
4. Will you actually build the agent? CloseBot rewards configuration work — objectives, knowledge, tools, testing. A sloppy build scales the sloppiness faster, as one reviewer put it. If nobody on your team will own that, a simpler product is the better decision.
5. Where do your clients' leads already live? If they're in GoHighLevel conversations and the problem is reply speed, CloseBot is the shorter path. If they're phone calls to a landline nobody answers after 5pm, Assistantz is solving the actual problem.

## FAQ

**Does CloseBot make AI voice calls?**
No. CloseBot is built for text-based channels inside your CRM — SMS, web chat, Messenger, email — with no phone or voice capability in its feature set or pricing. If voice is a requirement, Assistantz AI covers inbound and outbound calls.

**Is Assistantz AI cheaper than CloseBot?**
It depends on volume and whether the client needs voice. Assistantz starts at $197/mo and charges 7¢ per voice minute with your own key. CloseBot starts at $64/mo for a business plan bundling 500 messages, with a free tier underneath. For chat-heavy, no-voice use, CloseBot's entry cost is lower. For voice-heavy use, Assistantz is the only one of the two that can serve you at all.

**Can I white-label both and rebill clients?**
Yes. CloseBot's agency plan includes a white-label client portal and $0.012 per-message rebilling. Assistantz white-labels the dashboard on every listed plan and lets you rebill voice minutes, chat messages and phone numbers at your own markup.

**Does CloseBot have a free plan?**
Yes — 100 messages a month, one agent, one user seat, 1 MB of knowledge storage and unlimited account connections, free indefinitely. Paid plans also come with a 7-day trial before billing starts, and there are no refunds after that.

**Which one integrates better with GoHighLevel?**
CloseBot calls its HighLevel integration native and extends to HubSpot, LeadConnector and custom CRMs. Assistantz is GoHighLevel-specific and deploys assistants into sub-accounts white-labeled inside GHL. If GoHighLevel is your only stack, both work. If you run clients on HubSpot too, CloseBot covers more ground.

**Do I need my own API keys?**
For Assistantz, yes — the 7¢/minute and free-chat rates assume you bring your own OpenAI key and Twilio number. For CloseBot, the plans page says bring-your-own-key is not allowed for security reasons, while an older help article says V2 requires your own API keys. Confirm the current policy with CloseBot before you model your margin.
